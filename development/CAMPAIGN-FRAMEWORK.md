# Phase 2 — Playable Campaign Framework v0.1

**Status:** Design proposals for review; no campaign period, PC balancing rule, or new mechanic has been permanently adopted.  
**Basis:** World Builder & Game Master's Guide, printed pp. 3–11 (themes -> heroes -> iconic adventures -> minimal new rules), and `lore/research/SC98-MANUAL-AUDIT.md`.

## 1. What a Savage StarCraft campaign is
A roleplaying campaign about capable individuals and teams acting within a violent sector shaped by human political rivalries, radically different alien civilizations, advanced technology, psionics and biological horrors. The characters are not players who command endless RTS unit production; they are people (or exceptional beings) confronting missions, loyalties, survival challenges and larger events.

**Setting-level themes**
- **Terrans:** adaptability, expediency, fractured colonial governments, corporate rivalry, charismatic rebellion, professional and disposable soldiers, engineering.
- **Zerg:** invasive ecology, living war machines, evolution, asymmetric and sometimes centrally coordinated threats, terrifying loss of control.
- **Protoss:** ancient histories and fragmentary tradition, extraordinary psionics and technology, caste/tribe schisms, non-interference vs intervention, honor and political compromise.
- **Cross-cutting:** the costs of warfare, unreliable institutions, exploration of alien worlds, limited information, exceptional acts against overwhelming danger.

These themes are interpretive summaries anchored especially in SC98-MANUAL pp. 26–34, 51–55, 68–69 and 71–79; they are not new in-universe facts.

## 2. Campaign frameworks (same toolbox, different access and tone)

| Framework | Typical PCs | Core activities | Threat pressure | Initial implementation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Terran frontier / disaster investigation** | Marshal, marine veteran, technician, medic, scout, civilian survivor | Missing colony, quarantine, rescue, resources, evacuation, hard choices | Local corruption, Zerg infestation, isolation | **First pilot** |
| **Military special operations** | Infantry, support, command, reconnaissance, pilot, combat engineer | Recon, raids, fortified extraction, rescue, combined-arms missions | Heavier weapons, enemy squads/walkers, collateral damage | Second slice |
| **Mercenary and corporate work** | Contractor, miner, mechanic, security professional, smuggler | Contracts, escort, salvage, dealmaking, faction politics | Employers with conflicting objectives, scarce logistics | Low-prep sandbox |
| **Protoss expedition / sect politics** | Templar, Khalai specialist, pilgrim, explorer, investigator | Ancient sites, tech recovery, diplomacy, internal religious/political conflict | Psionic hazards, intrafaction tension, dangerous relics | Requires Protoss PC framework |
| **Ghost / intelligence thriller** | Operative, handler, analyst, infiltrator, extraction team | Covert action, espionage, compromised identities, sensitive technology | Concealment/countermeasures, political blowback | Requires psionics/cloaking |
| **Zerg-POV or exceptional swarm** | Intelligent controller or exceptional individualized Zerg | Direction, adaptation, conflicts of will, survival | Very asymmetric play and party agency | **Later design experiment** |
| **Sector-scale wartime command** | Officers, captains, civic leaders, taskforce representatives | Leadership, Mass Battles, space missions, strategy | Starships, planetary-scale consequences | After small-scale benchmarks |

**Do not** assume all seven use a common character power budget or all support unrestricted mixed-species parties.

## 3. Recommended *first* pilot frame
**Working location and era:** A Terran frontier mining/industrial settlement during the quieter interval following Brood War and preceding Wings of Liberty. **Provisional design selection**, not a canon fact or a final limitation on the larger setting.

**Why:** Mechanically accessible baseline (human PCs, equipment, investigation, Zerg), enough political variety to make a coherent party, readily expandable toward Protoss and capital ships without front-loading their rules.

**Alternative opening eras:**
- Original Confederacy / initial encounters (closely aligned with 1998 manual; regime and roster distinctly different).
- Brood War political chaos (UED, Dominion, independent remnants; continuity complexity).
- Later Dominion (modern in-setting equipment and post-WoL events; significant changes to Protoss and Zerg).

We should choose deliberately during pilot preparation. Beware **temporal leakage**: an SC98 source describes Confederate personnel/technology, not all Dominion forces several years later.

## 4. Who the heroes are: build concepts rather than classes
At least these **Terran proof-of-concept character concepts** should be buildable from SWADE + appropriately selected Sci-Fi Companion systems:
- Colonial marshal or security investigator.
- Frontier engineer/SCV technician and demolitions specialist.
- Medical professional or trained combat medic.
- Marine veteran / infantry specialist, with optional powered armor and varied loadouts.
- Scout/pilot/vehicle mechanic.
- Negotiator, contractor or logistics officer.
- Psychically gifted Ghost prototype or active covert operative (advanced/optional scope).

Role examples may become pregens, Professional Edges **only if a true design gap exists**, or GM-ready NPC profiles. Firebat, Marauder and Reaper are not mandatory PC classes. A detached flamethrower, powered suit or rocket launcher is still equipment.

**Protoss character scope** (research and design later): caste and tribe are sociocultural factors; psionic talent, technique, rank, armor and individual training are distinct. A Zealot is a fighter identity and loadout, not the sole permissible Templar build.

**Zerg scope:** Default Zergling/Hydralisk are usually GM-controlled organisms; an ordinary member of a directed swarm isn't assumed to possess PC-level free agency. Intelligent and exceptional individuals require intentional campaign premises.

## 5. Character power bands — provisional
1. **Frontier baseline:** Novice/Seasoned human Wild Cards; regular soldiers mostly Extras. Natural enemies are dangerous; teamwork/equipment important.
2. **Elite operatives:** Seasoned/Veteran Ghosts, veteran Templar or commandos; more specialized gear and psionics.
3. **Strategic/legendary:** High Templar paragons, major Zerg personalities, named canon figures. Not assumed to be ordinary starting PCs.

These are encounter design suggestions, **not** automatic statements about the canon rank of any named individual. SWADE rank measures game advancement, not military rank.

## 6. Tone and table promises
- Infantry firefights are dangerous; tactics, cover, support, traps, and specialized weapons count.
- Species differ drastically: recognize power asymmetry without denying PC agency.
- A conflict can be won by intelligence, rescue, evacuation or demolition instead of exterminating a swarm.
- Starships and Ultralisks are environmental/strategic threats until appropriate hardware or encounter scale exists.
- Psionics and alien equipment cannot be assumed interchangeable with Terran training.
- Campaign rules should remain transparent and SWADE-native.

## 7. What must be decided for the pilot
- Starting era choice and local sponsor of the team.
- Whether characters start with military combat suits or must requisition them.
- How much equipment/resource scarcity matters.
- Whether any PC has psionic power in the first iteration (recommend: no mandatory psi-PC).
- Threat-level expectations, character death tolerance, and mission objective priorities.
- Initial handling of infection risks, medical treatment and communications (no bespoke disease subsystem by default).

## 8. Minimum Phase 2 acceptance test
We can present 5 distinct character concepts, 3 scenario outlines with different problem types, one coherent equipment list and a pilot with meaningful noncombat choices, **without** creating a Firebat class or a full unit encyclopedia.

## 9. First proposed adventure modes
1. **The Silent Colony** (pilot): missing industrial outpost and suspected biological contamination.
2. **The Severed Supply Line:** factional sabotage and hostile mercenary pursuit.
3. **The Forbidden Signal:** alien transmission or relic forces an intelligence/ethical decision.

These are **original campaign concepts**, not StarCraft events; avoid representing them as established canon.
